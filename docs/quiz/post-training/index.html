Post-training

8 questions on Post-training, from 8 lessons. Pick an answer in your head, then open it.

On the roadmap for: AI Engineering.

  1. Question 1 of 8

    In supervised fine-tuning for chat, why is the loss masked on the prompt tokens?

    1. A. To save memory
    2. B. So the model learns to produce the responses, not to imitate users' prompts
    3. C. Prompts are random
    4. D. It isn't masked
    View answer

    ✓ B. So the model learns to produce the responses, not to imitate users' prompts

    Only the assistant's tokens are targets; the prompt is context.

    Lesson: SFT and Instruction Tuning

  2. Question 2 of 8

    How can a reward that isn't differentiable, such as 'the answer is correct', still train an LLM?

    1. A. It can't
    2. B. Policy gradients raise the probability of sampled outputs in proportion to their reward
    3. C. By converting the reward into a label
    4. D. By backpropagating through the grader
    View answer

    ✓ B. Policy gradients raise the probability of sampled outputs in proportion to their reward

    You only need the log-probability of what was sampled; a baseline reduces the variance of the updates.

    Lesson: Policy Gradients for LLMs

  3. Question 3 of 8

    What goes wrong if you optimise a learned reward model too hard?

    1. A. Nothing
    2. B. The policy finds the reward model's blind spots (reward hacking), so scores rise while real quality falls
    3. C. Training stops
    4. D. The reward model improves
    View answer

    ✓ B. The policy finds the reward model's blind spots (reward hacking), so scores rise while real quality falls

    A KL penalty to the reference model and early stopping limit this over-optimisation.

    Lesson: Reward Models and RLHF (PPO)

  4. Question 4 of 8

    How does DPO differ from RLHF with PPO?

    1. A. It uses more models
    2. B. It trains directly on preference pairs with a simple loss, with no separate reward model or RL loop
    3. C. It needs no preference data
    4. D. It only works on images
    View answer

    ✓ B. It trains directly on preference pairs with a simple loss, with no separate reward model or RL loop

    DPO's loss implicitly defines the reward, which makes training simpler and more stable.

    Lesson: DPO and Preference Optimisation

  5. Question 5 of 8

    In GRPO, used to train reasoning models, what replaces a learned value (critic) model?

    1. A. Human ratings
    2. B. Comparing several sampled answers to the same prompt and using each one's reward relative to the group average
    3. C. A second LLM
    4. D. No baseline at all
    View answer

    ✓ B. Comparing several sampled answers to the same prompt and using each one's reward relative to the group average

    The group mean acts as the baseline, which saves training a critic.

    Lesson: Reasoning Models: RLVR, GRPO and Test-Time Compute

  6. Question 6 of 8

    What does distillation mean for LLMs?

    1. A. Removing layers at random
    2. B. Training a smaller model on a larger model's outputs, such as its answers or reasoning traces
    3. C. Compressing the dataset
    4. D. Quantising weights
    View answer

    ✓ B. Training a smaller model on a larger model's outputs, such as its answers or reasoning traces

    Learning from a strong teacher's traces can beat training the small model with RL on its own.

    Lesson: Distillation: How Small Models Learn from Big Ones

  7. Question 7 of 8

    How does LoRA make fine-tuning cheap?

    1. A. It trains every weight in 8-bit
    2. B. It freezes the base weights and trains small low-rank matrices added to some layers
    3. C. It removes layers
    4. D. It skips the backward pass
    View answer

    ✓ B. It freezes the base weights and trains small low-rank matrices added to some layers

    Only a tiny fraction of parameters train. QLoRA also keeps the frozen base in 4-bit to fit on one GPU.

    Lesson: LoRA and QLoRA: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning

  8. Question 8 of 8

    Why do jailbreaks still work on safety-trained models?

    1. A. Safety training is never done
    2. B. Refusal training doesn't cover every phrasing; role-play, other languages or encodings can step outside it
    3. C. Models can't refuse
    4. D. Jailbreaks change the weights
    View answer

    ✓ B. Refusal training doesn't cover every phrasing; role-play, other languages or encodings can step outside it

    Defence needs several layers: training, input and output filters, and limiting what the model can do.

    Lesson: Safety Training and Jailbreaks
