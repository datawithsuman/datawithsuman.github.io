Training deep nets

10 questions on Training deep nets, from 11 lessons. Pick an answer in your head, then open it.

On the roadmap for: ML Engineering.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    Why does He initialisation scale weights by about √(2 / fan-in) for ReLU networks?

    1. A. To make weights positive
    2. B. To keep the variance of activations roughly constant from layer to layer, since ReLU zeroes half its inputs
    3. C. To speed up the GPU
    4. D. To avoid dropout
    View answer

    ✓ B. To keep the variance of activations roughly constant from layer to layer, since ReLU zeroes half its inputs

    With the wrong scale, signals shrink to zero or blow up across 20 layers before training even starts.

    Lesson: Weight Initialisation

  2. Question 2 of 10

    How do residual (skip) connections help very deep networks train?

    1. A. They add parameters
    2. B. They give gradients a direct path backwards through identity connections, so early layers keep learning
    3. C. They remove non-linearities
    4. D. They lower the learning rate
    View answer

    ✓ B. They give gradients a direct path backwards through identity connections, so early layers keep learning

    Each block learns a change to its input, and the identity path stops gradients from vanishing.

    Lesson: Vanishing & Exploding Gradients

  3. Question 3 of 10

    Why do Transformers use LayerNorm (or RMSNorm) instead of BatchNorm?

    1. A. BatchNorm is newer
    2. B. BatchNorm depends on batch statistics, which are unreliable with variable-length sequences and small batches; LayerNorm normalises each token on its own
    3. C. LayerNorm has no parameters
    4. D. BatchNorm can't be used on GPUs
    View answer

    ✓ B. BatchNorm depends on batch statistics, which are unreliable with variable-length sequences and small batches; LayerNorm normalises each token on its own

    Per-token normalisation behaves the same in training and inference, whatever the batch.

    Lesson: BatchNorm vs LayerNorm vs RMSNorm

  4. Question 4 of 10

    Why do LLM training runs warm up the learning rate at the start?

    1. A. To save electricity
    2. B. Early on, Adam's statistics are unreliable and the model is fragile, so large steps can destabilise training
    3. C. To make the loss rise
    4. D. Warm-up isn't used
    View answer

    ✓ B. Early on, Adam's statistics are unreliable and the model is fragile, so large steps can destabilise training

    Ramping the learning rate up over the first steps, then decaying it, gives stable and strong training.

    Lesson: Learning-Rate Schedules

  5. Question 5 of 10

    You increase the batch size 8×. What should usually happen to the learning rate?

    1. A. Keep it the same
    2. B. Increase it, roughly in proportion for SGD (the linear scaling rule), with warm-up
    3. C. Divide it by 8
    4. D. Set it to zero
    View answer

    ✓ B. Increase it, roughly in proportion for SGD (the linear scaling rule), with warm-up

    Larger batches give less noisy gradients, so bigger steps are safe, up to a limit.

    Lesson: Batch Size and Learning Rate

  6. Question 6 of 10

    How does dropout help a network generalise?

    1. A. It makes the network bigger
    2. B. Randomly dropping units stops them co-adapting and acts like averaging many thinner networks
    3. C. It speeds up inference
    4. D. It removes the need for data
    View answer

    ✓ B. Randomly dropping units stops them co-adapting and acts like averaging many thinner networks

    Activations are rescaled during training, so nothing changes at inference.

    Lesson: Dropout

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Why is Adam with L2 regularisation not the same as AdamW?

    1. A. They are the same
    2. B. With Adam, the L2 term passes through the adaptive scaling, so weights with large gradients are decayed less; AdamW decays weights directly
    3. C. AdamW has no weight decay
    4. D. L2 only works with SGD
    View answer

    ✓ B. With Adam, the L2 term passes through the adaptive scaling, so weights with large gradients are decayed less; AdamW decays weights directly

    Decoupling weight decay from the gradient update makes regularisation behave as intended.

    Lesson: Weight Decay vs L2, and AdamW

  8. Question 8 of 10

    What does mixup do?

    1. A. Shuffles labels
    2. B. Trains on blends of two examples and their labels, which smooths decision boundaries
    3. C. Mixes the train and test sets
    4. D. Averages model weights
    View answer

    ✓ B. Trains on blends of two examples and their labels, which smooths decision boundaries

    Like other augmentation, it creates new training signal without collecting data.

    Lesson: Data Augmentation and Mixup

  9. Question 9 of 10

    What does label smoothing change?

    1. A. It removes labels
    2. B. Targets become slightly soft (e.g. 0.9 instead of 1), which discourages over-confident predictions
    3. C. It adds more classes
    4. D. It averages predictions
    View answer

    ✓ B. Targets become slightly soft (e.g. 0.9 instead of 1), which discourages over-confident predictions

    Softer targets often improve calibration and generalisation.

    Lesson: Early Stopping and Label Smoothing

  10. Question 10 of 10

    Your model can't overfit a batch of just 8 examples. What does that tell you?

    1. A. You need more data
    2. B. There is likely a bug: wrong labels, a broken loss, frozen weights or a bad learning rate
    3. C. The model is too big
    4. D. Training is working well
    View answer

    ✓ B. There is likely a bug: wrong labels, a broken loss, frozen weights or a bad learning rate

    Any working model should memorise 8 examples; failing to is a quick bug detector.

    Lesson: Debugging Training: Loss Won't Go Down
